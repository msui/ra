Relationship Smorgasbord Quiz

This is a self-contained quiz that helps you and your partner(s) privately think through what you each want out of your relationship — things like emotional intimacy, physical affection, shared finances, or long-term commitment — without assuming a default relationship "package."

Download the quiz (html file)
  • Look for a file named something like relationship-anarchy-smorgasbord.html in your device's Downloads folder.
  • The download is a single HTML file. Open it in any browser (double-click it, or drag it into a browser window) — no install or internet connection required after the initial download.

Quick start guide

  1. Download the html quiz file and open it in your browser of choice. Hint—read the iOS or Android tips below if you're opening the quiz on a phone or tablet.
  2. Go through the quiz privately and rate each item.
  3. Everything runs locally in your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere (unless you share it manually).
  4. Export your results and send your answer file to your partner(s) however you'd like (text, AirDrop, email).
  5. One of you imports everyone's results files to their device to build a shared results page for discussion. (Or all of you can do this step invidually, if you each want a copy of the combined results on your device.)

Opening the quiz on a phone

How you open the file after downloading affects whether your progress reliably saves. On mobile, it works best to open it from your Files/Downloads app rather than straight from a browser preview.

iPhone

  1. Tap the download button above — Safari saves it to your Files app (usually in Downloads).
  2. Open the Files app and find it. Tapping it once may only open a "Quick Look" preview — don't rate the quiz from there. Tap the share icon and choose "Open in Safari" instead.
  3. Fill it out from there.

Android

  1. Tap the download button above — it'll download to your Downloads folder.
  2. Open it from Downloads (or the download notification) so it opens in the full Chrome app — not a "lite" in-app browser (like the one inside Gmail, Messages, or social apps), which won't reliably save your progress.
  3. Fill it out from there.

Doing it this way makes auto-saving quiz progress far more reliable. And if you get interrupted partway, you can hit Save My Progress inside the quiz to download a small file to resume from later.
